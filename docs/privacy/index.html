Privacy policy

Last updated: 5 November 2026 · App: Ohara (com.ember.ohara)

Ohara collects no personal data and sends nothing about you or your library to the developer or to any third party. Everything is stored on your phone.

What Ohara stores, and where

Your saved links, the article text extracted from them, your tags, highlights, notes, reading progress and settings are stored only in a database on your device. The developer has no server that receives this data and no copy of it.

What Ohara does not do

  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • No analytics, advertising, tracking or crash-reporting services.
  • No artificial-intelligence services: your content is not sent to any AI provider.
  • No selling or sharing of data, because none is collected.

Network requests Ohara makes

When you save a link, your phone requests that page directly from its own website, the same as opening it in a browser. That website, like any website you visit, can see the request from your device. For some links Ohara also reads public information from the source, such as a YouTube video's title and thumbnail, or the text export of a Google Doc that is shared publicly. Ohara makes no other network requests.

Backups and exports

Ohara can export your library as a file. The export happens only when you ask for it, and you choose where the file goes through your phone's share menu. Likewise, importing reads only a file you pick.

Separately, Android may include app data in your device's own backup to your Google account if you have Android backup switched on. That is a feature of your phone and your Google account, managed in your device settings. The developer does not receive it.

Deleting your data

Because the developer holds no copy, deleting your data is entirely in your hands: clear the app's storage in Android Settings, or uninstall Ohara. Either removes your library from the device. If you have exported files or Android backups, delete those too.

This website

This site is a static page hosted on GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics, and loads no third-party fonts or scripts. GitHub, as the host, may log technical data such as IP addresses to operate and secure its service, as described in GitHub's privacy statement.

Children

Ohara is a general-purpose reading tool. It collects no data from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If Ohara ever changes how it handles data, this page will be updated before the change is released, and the date above will change.

Contact

Questions or concerns: see the support page, or use the developer contact address on Ohara's Google Play listing.
