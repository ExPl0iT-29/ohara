Finally read the things you meant to read

Saving is easy. Getting back to it is the hard part. Ohara is built around the second half.

Saved in one tap

Share from Chrome, YouTube, Reddit or any app. Ohara keeps the link and pulls out the article on your phone, so saving is faster than leaving a tab open.

Better than the web page

No pop-ups, cookie banners or sidebars. Just the text, set for long reading, and it remembers exactly where you stopped.

Yours, and only yours

Everything is stored on your phone. There is no account and no server holding your reading list. Export it any time as a file you own.

How it works

Three steps, no setup.

  1. Share a link to OharaFrom any app's share menu, or paste a URL. Paste several at once to save a batch.
  2. Ohara prepares itArticles, YouTube videos, PDFs and public Google Docs are turned into a clean page in the background, on your device.
  3. Read whenever you likeOn the train, on a flight, anywhere. Tag it, save quotes with notes, search everything, and archive it when you're done.

What Ohara doesn't do

The short list of things we chose to leave out.

  • No account or sign-in
  • No ads, now or later
  • No analytics or tracking
  • No AI reading your articles
  • No streaks, badges or guilt
  • No cloud copy of your library

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. There are no ads, no subscription and no in-app purchases. Ohara has no servers, so it costs almost nothing to run.

I used Pocket or Omnivore. Can I move my links?

Yes. Ohara imports a plain list of links as a JSON file ([{"url": "…"}]). Convert your export to that shape, then use Settings → Import from file, and Ohara saves and prepares each link.

How do I back up my library or move to a new phone?

Settings → Export library creates a single file with everything: articles, tags, highlights and reading positions. Import it on the new phone. If Android backup is on, your phone may also include Ohara in its own backup to your Google account.

Does it work offline?

Once an item is prepared, its text is stored on your phone, so you can read it without a connection. Images load when you are online.

Is there an iPhone or desktop version?

Not yet. Ohara is Android-only for now.

What does Ohara do with my data?

Nothing, because it never receives any. Read the privacy policy; it's short.
